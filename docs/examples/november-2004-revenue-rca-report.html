Analysis Period: November 2004 vs. October 2004 · Date: November 19, 2026

Executive Summary

November 2004 gross merchandise revenue was $979,291.98, up 95.8% from October but down 0.9% from November 2003. Higher order volume drove the month-on-month increase, while lower average order value offset much of the benefit in both comparisons. November was the second-highest revenue month in the observed dataset and contributed 24.0% of available year-to-date revenue. Customer, product and geographic concentration cannot be assessed from the available aggregates.

Analysis & Findings

1. Trends and Period-over-Period Comparisons

October 2004 revenue was $500,233.86, with 13 orders, $38,479.53 average order value and 13 purchasing customers. November 2003 revenue was $988,025.15, with 30 orders, $32,934.17 average order value and 29 purchasing customers. November 2004 purchasing customers increased to 30, up 17 month over month and one year over year. Matched year-to-date revenue was $4,087,067.34, up $1,046,442.20 or 34.4% from $3,040,625.14 in the prior-year period; orders rose from 102 to 138 and distinct purchasing customers from 70 to 85. November ranked second among observed months and represented 24.0% of available 2004 year-to-date revenue. This history does not establish a repeatable seasonal pattern.

2. Primary Findings & Dimensional Drivers

The available evidence identifies order volume and average order value as the measurable commercial dimensions. An exact symmetric arithmetic bridge attributes $681,550.42 of the October-to-November increase to order volume and negative $202,492.30 to average order value, reconciling to the $479,058.12 increase. Against November 2003, the corresponding contributions were positive $93,914.53 and negative $102,647.70, reconciling to the $8,733.17 decline. Customer, product, product-line and country contributions, rankings and concentration are not estimable from the staged aggregates. Concentration therefore remains an unquantified risk, not an established exposure.

3. Root-Cause & Hypothesis Evaluation

The lower average order value is a verified arithmetic offset to higher order volume, but its underlying causes are not established. The bridge does not isolate pricing, product mix, commercial terms or customer behavior. No tested causal hypotheses were supplied. Management should investigate these possibilities before changing prices or promotions; the observed order increase alone also does not establish fulfillment risk.

Recommendations

1

Investigate lower average order value using order and product mix and commercial terms before changing prices or promotions.

2

Assess capacity against the higher order count and obtain status snapshots before diagnosing fulfillment exposure.

3

Recover complete customer, product, product-line and country aggregates, including entrants, exits and unknown categories; reconcile comparisons before assigning targeted actions or assessing concentration.

4

Confirm source completeness and accounting and currency definitions before external publication or planning around presumed seasonality.

Scope & Methodology Notes

  • Customer, product and geographic dimensions can overlap and should not be added together as independent revenue contributions.
  • The bridge is an arithmetic allocation, not evidence of causation; one prior November does not establish repeatable seasonality.
  • Observed dates do not establish source completeness, and matched year-to-date comparisons use available records rather than certified complete coverage.
  • No targets, margins, collections, foreign-exchange evidence or historical fulfillment snapshots were supplied. Observed status is not necessarily status at the order date.
